Clipper

A personal tool that turns long videos into short vertical clips and uploads them to its owner's own YouTube channel.

What it does

Clipper runs on its owner's computer. It watches a short list of video sources that permit reuse: videos published under a Creative Commons Attribution licence, public-domain footage, and creators' own clipping programmes. For each new video it makes a transcript, picks a few moments of 30 to 60 seconds, cuts them into captioned vertical clips, and writes a title and a description that credits the original video and its licence.

How it uses YouTube

Clipper uses YouTube API Services for two things: uploading those clips to the YouTube channel of the person who signs in, and reading that channel's name and the status and view counts of its videos. It does nothing else with a Google account.

Clipper is not offered to the public. It has no sign-up, no server and no user accounts: the only person who signs in is the person who runs it.

What Clipper accesses, where it is kept and how to remove it is set out in the privacy policy.
